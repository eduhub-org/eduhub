EduHub · Design review

Event page — critique & three directions

A review of /course/[id] rendered for a course whose Program.type = EVENTS, captured at 390 / 768 / 1440 px. The findings below are mostly not event-specific — spacing, alignment and heading hierarchy are shared by every program type, so fixing them here fixes the course and degree pages too.

Components reviewed: CourseContent/index.tsx · InfoPanel.tsx · Sessions.tsx · Registration/RegistrationButton.tsx · DescriptionFields.tsx

1 · What the current page does wrong

Layout & spacing

FindingWhy it hurts
No spacing scale The page uses ~15 distinct vertical values — space-y-24, mb-20, mb-24, mt-24, mt-12, mb-9, pb-12, mb-8, mb-6, mb-4, mb-3, p-12, sm:p-24, gap-y-8, gap-x-28. Nothing rhymes, so no gap reads as intentional and the eye can't tell "new section" from "next paragraph". high
Two gutters The hero title is inset p-3 (12px); everything below it is mx-6 (24px). Below 1280px the page title is visibly out of line with its own content column. med
items-center on a stacking row ContentRow is flex-col lg:flex-row; items-center was meant to centre the two columns vertically on desktop, but below lg it centres them horizontally. At 768px the tagline floats in the middle of the page, misaligned with every heading under it. high
Ragged right column CTA (justify-center), deadline text, guest link and the white info card each centre themselves independently inside lg:max-w-md. At 1440px the card's left edge and the button's left edge are 90px apart — four fragments, no shared edge. med
Half-empty info card p-12 sm:p-24 + grid-cols-2 gap-x-28 on three items: at 1440px, ~44% of the card is blank white and "11.09.2026 – 18.09.2026" is forced onto three lines by a 112px column gap. On mobile 96px of padding leaves a 200px content well. high
Dead zones 147px between hero and tagline, ~190px of empty dark between the tagline row and "Programm", ~100px between the info card and the description card. The page carries ~1400px of content across 2600px of scroll. med
Redundant nesting Two nested <div class="flex flex-col space-y-12 lg:space-y-24"> with identical classes; the outer one wraps a single child. index.tsx mixes PageBlock, ContentRow and raw mx-6 xl:mx-0 for the same job. low

Information architecture

FindingWhy it hurts
The answer is 1400px down On mobile the two facts a visitor came for — when and where — first appear in the white card below the agenda. The hero carries a title and nothing else. high
Dates stated twice, differently The info card says "11.09.2026 – 18.09.2026"; the agenda immediately above says "Freitag, 11.09.2026" and "Freitag, 18.09.2026". A span implies a continuous event; it is actually two evenings a week apart. med
Description is last and loudest DescriptionFields — the only copy that says what the event is — renders after the agenda, after the info card, in a full-bleed bg-edu-course-invited block. That token is --eduhub-status-invited: the same orange used for the "you are invited" enrollment state and for the primary CTA. Semantic colour used as decoration. high
Registration deadline outlives the event The page cheerfully shows "Anmeldeschluss: 31.10.2026" for an event that ends 18.09.2026. Nothing clamps or warns. Once the event is over the page still reads as open. med
No status, no scarcity The tile carries a "Vergangen" badge; the page does not. maxParticipants / activeParticipantCount already drive the waitlist but are never surfaced — no "12 Plätze frei", no "noch 3 Tage". med
People land in two places SessionSpeakers render as UserCards inside agenda rows; CourseInstructors render in the white card. Same entity, two treatments, no "Speakers" section. low

Typography, colour & components

FindingWhy it hurts
Page title = section title The hero title is text-3xl. SectionTitle ("Programm") is also text-3xl font-semibold. The name of the event and a subhead are the same size — against a brand whose own guidance calls for "huge hero type". high
No <h1> anywhere The title renders as a plain <div>. curl of the page returns 0 <h1>. Day headings jump straight to <h3>. Screen-reader users get no document title, and the JSON-LD/SEO work in 55c6070a is undercut by the markup it sits in. high
Day vs. item only 2px apart Day heading text-lg sm:text-xl (20px) vs. session title text-sm sm:text-lg (18px). The agenda's two levels barely separate; times in full label-primary white compete with titles. med
Time column isn't a column The time block is flex-shrink-0 with no fixed width, so "19:00 - 21:00" and "21:00 - 23:00" render at slightly different widths and the titles beside them don't align. low
Off-brand buttons "Zum Kalender hinzufügen (.ics)" is a grey rounded-lg rectangle on bg-secondary; the brand's buttons are pills with a 2px border that turns teal on hover. "(.ics)" is developer jargon in learner-facing copy. low
Three accents competing Orange CTA, orange description block, teal guest link — and the brand has exactly one accent (#00A398). The CTA also carries shadow-lg hover:-translate-y-0.5, a lift the design system explicitly rules out ("press: colour change only"). med
Empty session row A session without a title renders as a lone "21:00 - 23:00" next to nothing. Looks broken rather than terse. low
⚑
Not a bug: the small circular “N” in the bottom-left of the screenshots is the Next.js dev-tools indicator, not page chrome.

3 · The shared spacing scale

All three directions below are built on the same five-step rhythm. This is the part worth landing first, because it is program-type agnostic: the same tokens fix the course and degree pages.

Section · 96 / 64Between top-level sections (hero → about → agenda → description). 64 under lg.
Block · 32Heading → its content, and between sibling blocks inside a section.
Item · 16Between rows in a list: agenda slots, fact rows, speakers.
Tight · 8Label → value, chip gaps, icon → text.
Gutter · 24One page inset, applied to the hero title too — so the title and the content below share an edge at every width.
→
Concretely: delete the nested space-y-12 lg:space-y-24 wrapper, give PageBlock the gutter as its single responsibility, drop items-center from ContentRow (or make it lg:items-center), and replace every ad-hoc mt-24 / mb-20 / mb-9 / pb-12 with section / block.

4 · Direction A — Tighten

low riskno new componentsapplies to all program types

Same structure, same components, same reading order. Only the rhythm, the alignment and the two cards change. The white info panel becomes a labelled fact list instead of a 2×2 icon grid with 112px gutters, the description block loses the status-orange, and the hero title becomes a real <h1> at hero scale. This is the version that ships in an afternoon.

5 · Direction B — Event-first hero

medium riskevent-specific herokills the info card

The hero answers the question. Title at hero scale with a fact strip — date, time, place, language, status — directly under it, so on any device the first screen says what, when, where. The white info panel disappears entirely; its contents were only ever those facts plus the organiser, who moves to a proper Speakers section. Body becomes one wide column, and a slim sticky bar carries the CTA once the hero scrolls past.

6 · Direction C — Sticky registration rail

higher riskconventional (Luma / Eventbrite)best for conversion

Content left, a single sticky card right. That card absorbs everything the current page scatters across four fragments: CTA, deadline, guest link, facts, calendar export, share. It follows the scroll, so registration is always one click away no matter how long the agenda. On mobile the rail collapses into one compact card directly under the hero — which incidentally is also the fix for the "when/where is 1400px down" problem, without a bespoke hero.

7 · Recommendation

Ship A first, then C. A is pure hygiene — the spacing scale, the single gutter, the <h1>, the items-center fix — and every line of it benefits the course and degree pages too, so it is the cheapest way to raise the floor across all program types. C is the structural answer: it solves the "facts are below the agenda" problem and the "CTA scrolls away" problem with one component, and its rail degrades naturally to the existing white card for non-events.

B's hero fact-strip is the most striking of the three, but it buys the least that C doesn't already buy, at the cost of a second hero variant to maintain. Worth keeping in the drawer as a follow-up for high-profile events (Coding.Waterkant and friends), where a full-bleed hero with the date in it earns its keep.

Independent of the direction, four things should be fixed regardless: (1) the missing <h1>, (2) the description block's use of the invited status orange, (3) a registration deadline that may fall after the event ends, and (4) the untitled session rendering as an empty row.
