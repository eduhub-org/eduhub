The projects overview lists projects of many courses in one place. Open it from the menu item **Projects** in the **Management** section.

- **Admins** see every project of every course.
- **Org Admins** who may manage courses or events (**Can manage courses**, **Can manage events**) see it too. They see the projects listed in the courses of the programs they manage: course programs with **Can manage courses**, event programs with **Can manage events**, degree programs with **Can manage degrees**. Projects of other organizations don't appear, and the **Program** filter only offers their own programs. Instructors don't see the overview; they find their projects on the projects tab of their course's manage page.

## What the list shows

Each row is one project, with its **Status**, **Title**, **Course(s) / Program** (every course the project is listed in, with the short title of the course's program), **Authors** and **Type**.

The list is sorted by Project Status in the order of the project lifecycle: Project Template, Awaiting Confirmation, Ongoing, Sent Back, Submitted, Passed, Suggested for Publication, Published, Not Passed. Within a status, the most recently changed projects come first.

The list shows 50 projects per page. Use the arrows below the list to move between pages, or change **Items per page**.

## Finding projects

Above the list there is one chip per Project Status: **Project Template**, **Awaiting Confirmation**, **Ongoing**, **Sent Back**, **Submitted**, **Passed**, **Suggested for Publication**, **Published** and **Not Passed**. Each chip shows how many projects have that status. Click chips to show only projects with those statuses; you can select several at once, and clicking a selected chip deselects it.

The overview opens on **Needs Attention**: the projects waiting for a decision from you. For Admins these are the statuses Awaiting Confirmation (Project Confirmation), Submitted (review) and Suggested for Publication. For Org Admins they are Awaiting Confirmation and Submitted, because only Admins publish projects. Click **Show all** to clear the selection and see every project, and **Needs Attention** to get back to it.

You can narrow the list further:

- **Program**: projects listed in at least one course of that program.
- **Course group**: projects listed in at least one course of that course group.
- **Search**: finds projects whose title, tagline or description, author or mentor names, course title or program short title contain every word you type.

The chip counts take the program, course group and search into account, so they show how many projects of each status match those filters.

### Searching by meaning

A search of three or more characters also finds projects by meaning, not only by the words you typed: searching for "renewable energy", for example, also finds a project about solar boats. The results then form one list sorted by relevance instead of by status: projects found both by their words and by their meaning come first, followed by the others in order of how well they match. A note above the list says that it is sorted by relevance.

The status chips, program and course group still apply, and the chip counts show how many of the results have each status. The meaning of a project comes from its title, tagline and description; changes to these are picked up the next day. You only find projects you can also see in the normal list.

If a search finds more keyword matches than can be sorted, a note tells you to refine your search. If the search by meaning is unavailable, a note says so and you only see keyword matches.

Shorter searches (one or two characters) only look for the words and keep the normal order by status.

## Working on a project

Click a row to expand it. You get the same actions as on the projects tab of a course's manage page: confirming a project (Project Confirmation), reviewing a submitted project, adding and removing authors and mentors, editing the title, tagline, description, cover image, type and documentation instruction, and suggesting a project for publication. Your changes are saved straight away.

**Open project page** opens the Project Page.

Adding a project to a course and taking a project out of a course are only possible on the manage page of that course.

Since a project can be listed in several courses, the overview doesn't know which course's submission deadline applies. It only shows a deadline set on the project itself; the course or program deadline is shown on the course's manage page.

## Preview and publishing

**Preview** shows a project the way its Project Page presents it: cover image, tagline, description, authors and, for a Passed Project, its documentation, presentation and link.

Admins publish a project in the public showcase with **Publish**, and take it out again with **Unpublish**. Both are offered for Passed Projects (except online courses), open Project Templates and projects that are already published. Publishing fulfils a suggestion for publication; unpublishing leaves the project's status otherwise unchanged. Org Admins can't publish projects. The same buttons are on the projects tab of a course's manage page.

A project can only be published once its Project Team has agreed to publication. The team decides when it submits the project and can change its mind later; below the title you see **Publication approved** or **Publication declined**. Without the team's consent **Publish** is greyed out, with a note that the team hasn't agreed. The platform refuses such a publication everywhere, not only on this page. **Unpublish** is always possible.

Project Templates have no team and can always be published, for example so that a company finds participants for its topic. When you add a project without authors on a course's manage page, which makes it a Project Template, a note tells you it can be published in the showcase.

If a team withdraws its consent while its project is published, the project is taken out of the showcase straight away. Whenever a team has withdrawn its consent, its row shows a warning: **The team withdrew its publication consent on** the date. The warning disappears once the team agrees again. The status shows the project's normal Project Status.

Suggesting a project for publication stays possible without the team's consent, so you can still talk to the team about it.

Above the list, Admins find links to the settings **Project types** and **Documentation instructions**. Org Admins don't see these links.
